# How to Fix the Web

In March 2026, John Carvalho, writing as CEO of Synonym, published [How to Fix Spam, Bots, and AI Slop on the World Wide Web](https://howtofixtheweb.com/). The subtitle on the page is “The Semantic Social Graph Is the Real Algorithm.” He also posted the URL from his Pubky: [link post](https://pubky.app/post/gujx6qd8ksydh1makdphd3bxu351d9b8waqka8hfg6q7hnqkxexo/0035KD6FWG9C0). This page reports that essay. See [[John Carvalho]].

## What he argues

John Carvalho argues that major platforms decide relevance from the center, and that asking for a better algorithm leaves that control in place. He argues that spam is not a global category of bad content. Spam, in the essay, is unwanted reach: the same message can be useful to one person and noise to another. The question he poses is whether the content has earned a credible path to this user in this context.

He describes a **semantic social graph** as a graph whose nodes are public keys, content, and other addressable targets, and whose edges are tags users apply. He says trust in that graph is contextual, graduated, and directional, and that graph distance determines reach. He writes: “This is the system we are building at Synonym through Pubky.”

He treats four responses to openness and noise: fully open, fully closed, a central algorithm, and contextual trust. He argues that the fourth is what the semantic social graph enables, and that it avoids both an unfiltered firehose and a walled garden.

On predecessors, he argues that PGP’s Web of Trust showed trust can propagate without a central authority, but was binary and hard to use; that the Semantic Web wanted links to carry meaning; and that platform social graphs made relationships computable and then captured them. He calls the semantic social graph the combination of those three, under user sovereignty.

He argues that a central feed flattens context, creates an arms race with spammers, and makes users dependent on logic they cannot inspect or take with them. He defines **credible exit** in this essay as leaving a platform, host, or service and taking identity, data, relationships, and relevance logic. The knowledge-base page [[CredibleExit|Credible Exit]] uses the phrase for Pubky data portability. The definition in this paragraph is his essay’s.

The requirements he lists are: anchor identity to keys rather than leased accounts; make tags the edges; filter by trust paths rather than a global model; make the graph portable; and keep the protocol open so one company does not own the graph. He says this is what “we” are building, that the semantic social graph is live, and that what remains is adoption. That is his claim about the state of the work.
