The same public key, `gujx6qd8ksydh1makdphd3bxu351d9b8waqka8hfg6q7hnqkxexo`, has a [Pubky profile](https://pubky.app/profile/gujx6qd8ksydh1makdphd3bxu351d9b8waqka8hfg6q7hnqkxexo) whose name is John Carvalho and whose bio reads: `` `CEO` at `Synonym`, creators of `Pubky`, `Bitkit`, and more. `` The GitHub account [BitcoinErrorLog](https://github.com/BitcoinErrorLog) uses the name John Carvalho, the bio “CEO at Synonym.to,” the company “Synonym,” and the blog `https://synonym.to`.

That is the first-party public identity used below. Arguments in the linked works are his, not settled descriptions of Synonym products.

## Public work index

### Conservation of Blockspace

[[Conservation of Blockspace]] records his May 2026 paper *Credible Exit and the Law of Conservation of Blockspace* (v1.10.3) and the site [blockspace.science](https://blockspace.science/). The public repository is [BitcoinErrorLog/conservation-of-blockspace](https://github.com/BitcoinErrorLog/conservation-of-blockspace), described there as “Research on Bitcoin scaling limitations.”

### Web of trust, keys, and sovereignty

- [[How to Fix the Web]] — March 2026 essay, bylined John Carvalho, CEO, Synonym. He argues that spam is unwanted reach, that relevance should follow a portable semantic social graph of keys and tags, and that this is what Synonym is building through Pubky. He also links the essay from his Pubky collection: [howtofixtheweb.com](https://howtofixtheweb.com/) and [the link post](https://pubky.app/post/gujx6qd8ksydh1makdphd3bxu351d9b8waqka8hfg6q7hnqkxexo/0035KD6FWG9C0).
- In a [Bitfinex interview](https://blog.bitfinex.com/education/what-is-pubky/), John Carvalho, identified there as CEO of Synonym, says Pubky uses the public key as a domain name, which he calls PKDNS, placed on Mainline DHT. He says Pubky is part of Synonym’s “Atomic Economy” vision and lists Bitkit, Blocktank, Paykit, and Atomicity in that vision. Those are his statements in the interview.
- [How to Think About Names](https://pubky.app/post/gujx6qd8ksydh1makdphd3bxu351d9b8waqka8hfg6q7hnqkxexo/0035R8SA18FCG) opens in his voice: a name can be copied without being taken, and a lie about identity is a different act.
- [Exit-Ordered Role Allocation for Recovery and Continuity](https://pubky.app/post/gujx6qd8ksydh1makdphd3bxu351d9b8waqka8hfg6q7hnqkxexo/0035JSEN5F7KG) is his Pubky essay on recovery roles. He argues that assigning each recovery role to a specific person or device at setup creates a durable mapping an attacker can learn. The public GitHub essay [The Keys That Do Not Exist Yet](https://github.com/BitcoinErrorLog/ideas/blob/main/exit-ordered-role-allocation/the-keys-that-do-not-exist-yet.md), in [BitcoinErrorLog/ideas](https://github.com/BitcoinErrorLog/ideas), states a narrower claim: a system can commit to a bounded sequence of roles before it commits those roles to individual terminal controllers. The repository README describes that line of work as exit-ordered role allocation.

The knowledge base page [[CredibleExit|Credible Exit]] describes a Pubky property. John’s paper and the March 2026 essay use the same phrase for their own definitions, linked from [[Conservation of Blockspace]] and [[How to Fix the Web]].

### Bitcoin and value

- [Settlement is Subjective](https://pubky.app/post/gujx6qd8ksydh1makdphd3bxu351d9b8waqka8hfg6q7hnqkxexo/0035HNVJEEBXG). John Carvalho argues that confirmations are evidence about a state change, and that settlement is decided by the people in the real-world deal.
- [You Cannot Store “Lifetime”](https://pubky.app/post/gujx6qd8ksydh1makdphd3bxu351d9b8waqka8hfg6q7hnqkxexo/0035JX1KFXSRG). He argues against treating money as stored lifetime, on the ground that value is subjective and there is no objective value of a person’s time to preserve.
- The blockspace paper, above, is his separate argument that unilateral Bitcoin exit is a finite block-weight window, not a claim that layers are useless.
